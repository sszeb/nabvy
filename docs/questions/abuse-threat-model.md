# Questions from the abuse threat model session (4.3t)

- **2026-09-24, 4.3t: free want radius versus the burst's cost.** The owner lets each want's radius be set freely; a free want over 40 areas makes each burst check 40 times the lone cost, so one account drains the free pool 40 times as fast before its £2 cap stops it. Option taken (G8, 7.1b): a free window's cost stays that of the policy's shape whatever the radius, by slowing the cadence as areas grow; the radius itself is never changed. Conservative because it keeps the free tier's spend what the policy costed, and changes no wording or price.
- **2026-09-24, 4.3t: the free pool against the monthly Apify cap.** The pool's floor of £20 a day is about £600 a month; the gateway's hard cap is $150 (about £120) a month for all users. Option taken (G7, 1.6g): until the owner sets it, the free pool's monthly ceiling is half the provider monthly cap, and a day's pool is the smaller of £20 and what remains of that ceiling. Conservative because paid watchers keep at least half the month's provider budget. Owner, 2026-09-24: the free tier is limited; paid users may sign up and upgrade at any time. That confirms the direction; the ceiling's share is still open.
- **2026-09-24, 4.3t: the user's monthly spending limit.** G9 proposes a monthly spending limit each user sets, on by default, as leading consumer apps do for usage billing. The default amount is a product decision; option taken: the default equals the user's last top-up, editable at any time. Conservative because it never spends more than the user has put in within a month without their action.
- **2026-09-24, 4.3t: referral dispute window.** G13 pays a referral credit only after the referee's first paid purchase clears its dispute window. Option taken: 14 days after the payment. Conservative because it is shorter than a card chargeback window but longer than most fraud-driven refunds; the owner may prefer the full window.
